## Report Structure

# [Company Name] ([Ticker]) Due Diligence & FMV

## 1. Business Overview
- What the company does
- Main business segments
- Revenue breakdown
- Geographic exposure
- Competitive position
- Key customers or industries served

## 2. Investment Thesis
Main bullish and bearish points in short bullet format.

## 3. Business Classification & Valuation Framework
Classify the company (mature compounder, cyclical, hypergrowth, SaaS, industrial, semiconductor, AI infrastructure, consumer platform, defensive, asset-heavy, capital-light, etc.). Explain what type of business it is, what ultimately drives its valuation, and which company-specific factors justify the selected framework.

Select the most relevant metrics for the industry and explain WHY they matter. Do not force irrelevant metrics; do not blindly apply software multiples to industrial companies. Examples:
- SaaS → EV/Sales, Rule of 40, FCF margin
- Industrials → EV/EBITDA, FCF, margins
- Semis → Forward P/E, margins, cycle timing
- Banks → P/B, ROE
- Consumer → EPS growth, margins, brand power

## 4. Financials & Financial Quality
- Revenue growth (5Y if available), EPS growth
- Gross / operating / net margins and margin trends
- Free cash flow, FCF margins, cash conversion
- Cash & debt, balance sheet strength
- Share dilution or buybacks
- ROIC / ROE, operating leverage
- Current guidance; analyst estimates if widely available
- Trailing-twelve-month (TTM) figures for revenue, margins, FCF, and EPS — not just the last completed fiscal year. Show the last 2–4 quarters individually so the margin and growth trajectory into the present is visible, not just the annual average.

Determine: strong business quality vs cyclical risk, durable moat, pricing power, capital allocation strength. Focus heavily on trends and financial quality.

## 5. Competitive Advantages
Durable advantages: scale, switching costs, brand, network effects, manufacturing, distribution, patents/IP, cost advantages. Evaluate market share trajectory — gaining, defending, or losing share — plus industry structure and disruption risk.

## 6. Growth Drivers
Primary drivers of future upside (AI/datacenter demand, cloud migration, pricing power, international expansion, product cycles, margin expansion, acquisitions, etc.). Separate secular growth, cyclical growth, and temporary tailwinds.

## 7. Valuation & FMV Analysis
Current multiples: market cap, enterprise value, P/E, forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, PEG, price to sales, FCF yield. Compare against the company's own historical ranges when available.

Estimate fair value using multiple methodologies where appropriate (forward P/E, EV/EBITDA, DCF, EV/Sales, FCF yield, sum-of-the-parts, own historical ranges). Provide bear, base, and bull cases, each with revenue growth, margin, terminal multiple, and macro assumptions.

For high-growth names (forward EPS growth above ~25%), anchor the base-case multiple to forward growth via PEG (~1.0–1.25 for high-quality growers) rather than purely to the company's historical multiple range — a historical range from a slower-growth era will systematically undervalue a structurally changed grower. State the PEG explicitly so the growth credit is visible and auditable.

Use historical annual data for context and trend analysis, AND build every case's revenue, margin, and cash-flow inputs from TTM figures and the most recent 2–4 quarters — never from the last completed fiscal year alone. The most recent data must never be omitted from the valuation inputs. If a case assumes margins or growth rates that contradict what the most recent quarters show (e.g. normalizing margins down while they are expanding), state the override explicitly and justify it with evidence; do not silently discard the recent data.

Do not compare valuation multiples, FMV, premiums, or discounts against peers, competitors, industry averages, or any other stocks. Cross-stock analysis belongs in the separate stock comparison researcher.

## 8. Buy Zone Framework
Generate Strong Buy / Fair Buy / Expensive / Overextended zones based on valuation multiples, historical ranges, growth-adjusted valuation, and the macro/rate environment. Explain what conditions would justify each zone.

## 9. Risks
Material risks only, separated:
- Business risks: industry, competition, cyclicality, regulation, customer concentration, debt, execution, margin pressure, disruption
- Valuation risks: multiple compression, unrealistic growth expectations, hype, macro slowdown, margin normalization

## 10. Management
CEO background, founder-led or not, capital allocation quality, notable insider ownership, historical execution.

## 11. Key Metrics To Watch
The most important KPIs investors should monitor quarterly.

## 12. Final Verdict
Concise and balanced:
- Whether the stock is undervalued, fairly valued, or overvalued, and whether the current valuation is justified by fundamentals
- Overall business quality and main financial strengths/weaknesses
- Key drivers for long-term compounding, what could break the thesis, expected long-term return profile
- Distinguish between a great business and a great stock at the current valuation

Consider the macroeconomic environment and interest rates. Penalize low-quality earnings and highly cyclical businesses; reward durable moats, pricing power, strong ROIC/FCF, recurring revenue, and strong balance sheets. Use realistic assumptions, not optimistic projections. Include qualitative reasoning alongside quantitative valuation.

Keep the entire report concise, information-dense, and professional. Prioritize financials and valuation over narrative commentary.
